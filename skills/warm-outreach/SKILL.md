# Warm Outreach

People reply to messages about their own situation, in their own words, that ask for one small thing. They ignore pitches, credentials and lists of problems. Done means one message, usually under 80 words, that the sender can send as is, plus the follow-up plan with dates.

The first-touch method adapts Boring Marketing's `pitch-the-fix` (MIT, see THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). The conversation rules come from calibrated-question negotiation (Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference*).

## Hard rules

1. Never send. Draft, show, and wait for an explicit yes.
2. No fake urgency, no fake "Re:", no invented familiarity. Loss framing only on true facts.
3. Use the exact address or handle that was published or already in the thread. Watch for impersonator accounts on social platforms: reply only to the verified one.

## First touch (someone who hasn't heard of you)

Pick one thing that passes four tests: they can see it in ten seconds, it costs them customers, you can fix it in days, and you can show it worked within about a month with a number they already trust.

Shape: who you are in half a sentence and what you looked at → the one thing, in their own words where possible → what it costs them, about a customer not a statistic → the fix, scoped, with a timeframe → one low-effort question ("Want me to send the screenshot?"). No link or attachment in a first email.

## Warm threads: the rules

- **Answer the question they asked, with a specific yes.** If they say "can you meet tomorrow, I'm free all day", reply with a time, not a booking link. A link turns their yes into homework.
- **Label before you ask.** "It sounds like the week got away from you." Never follow a label with "but".
- **Mirror their exact words.** Quote a phrase they used back to them; it shows you listened.
- **One no-oriented ask.** "Would Monday be a bad time?" gets more replies than "Let me know when works". People say no more easily than yes, and a no keeps them talking.
- **Own it when you fumbled.** One sentence, no grovelling: "I sent a link instead of just saying yes, that's on me."
- **End with one question, then stop typing.** Nothing after the question.
- **Match the channel.** A DM is two to four sentences. Voice-note ping-pong loses momentum: move to a call with a date.
- **Send the brand link that matches the conversation.** A prospect hiring a builder should land on the services page, not a different business's page.

## Recovery and follow-ups

| When | Message |
|---|---|
| They went quiet after showing intent | Own the miss in one line, quote their words, offer two specific times, one question |
| No reply after ~7 days | One line, whole message: "Has this fallen off your list?" |
| Still nothing | Stop. A quiet close keeps the door open; a third chase shuts it |

## Before sending, check

One point? Their words quoted? One question, at the end? Specific time instead of a link when they offered availability? No pressure that isn't true? Would the sender say this out loud to them? If any answer is no, rewrite.
