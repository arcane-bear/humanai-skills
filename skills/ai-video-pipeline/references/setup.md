# AI video pipeline: setup reference

Load this when you are actually building the pipeline. Facts, menu paths and prices were checked against the vendors' own docs on 2026-11-25. Anything older than about 90 days: re-check the linked source before relying on it, and say so if you couldn't.

## 1. Decide: film yourself, AI footage, or a mix

**Goal:** Pick the cheapest pipeline that still sounds and looks like your business, and know what it will cost each month.

**Time:** about 12 minutes. **Needs:** Claude, Higgsfield, HeyGen, CapCut, Your phone

**Steps (the path that works):**

1. Answer one question honestly: do customers buy from you because of you (coach, consultant, tradesperson, clinic owner)? If yes, film yourself on your phone. Your face IS the brand, so an avatar or AI clip will feel like a stand-in.
2. If your product, process or results are the star (software, food, before/after jobs, explainers), AI visuals can carry the video. Your voice or on-screen text does the talking.
3. Pick one of three setups. (A) Phone + Claude: you film, Claude writes. (B) Phone + Higgsfield B-roll: you film the talking parts, Higgsfield generates cutaway clips. (C) Avatar: a HeyGen or Higgsfield talking avatar made from YOUR OWN face and voice, for weeks you can't film.
4. Price your setup with the official list prices (checked 2026-11-25, USD, monthly billing, before tax). Claude Pro is $20/mo ($17/mo billed annually). Higgsfield's plans are Basic (120 credits a month), Plus (1,000) and Ultra (3,000 and up). On 25 Nov 2026 they were $9, $25 and $45 a month billed yearly, but that was a Black Friday sale; Plus normally lists at $49 a month billed yearly. HeyGen Creator is $29/mo for 600 credits. CapCut Pro is about $19.99/mo, or $0 if YouTube's own auto captions are enough for you.
5. Illustrative monthly totals, not quotes: Setup A is about $20-40. Setup B is about $40-100, depending on how many AI clips you generate. Setup C is about $50-70. Start on the smallest plan and upgrade only when you run out of credits two months in a row.
6. Write your choice and your monthly budget cap on one line, for example: 'Setup B, cap $60/month, review in 8 weeks.'

**Done when:** You can say in one sentence which setup you're using, why it fits your business, and your monthly spend cap.

**Watch out:** Prices change often and promotions are common. The Higgsfield pricing page was showing a Black Friday sale when checked, so treat the Higgsfield figures as sale prices and expect the regular price to be higher. Higgsfield credits per video vary by model, and the pricing page doesn't give a per-video cost, so we haven't estimated one. Recheck each pricing page before you subscribe.

## 2. Turn your notes into hooks and scripts with Claude

**Goal:** Turn one messy idea into five ready-to-record short scripts, each with a strong first line, in under 20 minutes.

**Time:** about 15 minutes. **Needs:** Claude

**Steps (the path that works):**

1. Collect raw material: three to five real customer questions, objections or mistakes you heard this month. Voice notes, emails and reviews all count. Paste them as they are, without polishing.
2. Open Claude and paste the prompt below. Fill in every [bracket]. The more specific your notes, the less generic the scripts.
3. Ask Claude for five scripts from the one idea, each with three hook options. Choose the hook you would actually say out loud to a customer.
4. Read each script aloud with a timer. Cut anything you wouldn't say in person. Aim for 30-45 seconds, or roughly 75-110 spoken words.
5. Fact-check every claim, price and number yourself. Claude drafts. You are responsible for what goes out under your name.
6. Save your best prompt and your edited scripts in one doc so next week starts from a template, not a blank page.

**Prompt: Weekly hooks + scripts prompt**

```
You are my short-form video scriptwriter. I run [business type] in [city/region] for [ideal customer]. My tone is [e.g. plain-spoken, warm, a bit dry]. Never use hype words like 'game-changer', 'insane' or 'secret'.

Here are my raw notes from this week (customer questions, mistakes I see, things I explained more than once):
[paste notes]

Turn the single strongest idea in these notes into 5 short vertical videos for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok. For each video give me:
1. Three hook options (first line spoken in under 3 seconds). Mix one question, one bold plain statement, and one 'mistake' hook.
2. A script of 75-110 spoken words that I can say naturally to camera, using short sentences and one idea per video.
3. A one-line on-screen text overlay for the first 2 seconds.
4. Two or three B-roll shot ideas I could film on my phone OR generate as AI clips (label which is which).
5. A soft call to action that fits [goal: e.g. book a call / visit the shop / follow for part 2].

Rules: use only facts from my notes or general common knowledge; if you need a number or claim I didn't give you, write [CHECK] instead of inventing it. Write in [UK/US] English. End with a table of the 5 titles (under 60 characters each).
```

**Done when:** You have five scripts you've read aloud, each under 45 seconds, with every [CHECK] resolved or removed.

**Watch out:** Claude's free plan can do this. Pro ($20/mo when checked) gives you more usage for weekly batching. Never paste customers' personal details, such as names, phone numbers or health information, into any AI tool.

## 3. Make the footage: phone, Higgsfield, or an avatar

**Goal:** Produce the visuals for this week's five scripts using the setup you picked in Lesson 1.

**Time:** about 20 minutes. **Needs:** Your phone, Higgsfield, HeyGen

**Steps (the path that works):**

1. Phone first, if that's your setup. Film vertically (9:16) at eye level, facing a window for light, about an arm's length away. Use a cheap clip-on mic if you have one. Record all five scripts in one sitting and do each hook twice.
2. For cutaway clips in Higgsfield, open its video generation and write a plain shot description from your script's B-roll list, for example: 'close-up of hands sanding a wooden table, warm workshop light, vertical'. Generate, then keep only the clips that look like your real world.
3. Don't generate fake versions of your real premises, products, staff or customer results. Use AI for illustrative or abstract shots, and film the real thing yourself.
4. For an avatar, use ONLY your own face and voice. Higgsfield's AI Talking Avatar turns a portrait plus a script or audio into a lip-synced presenter video, and Higgsfield says it is for 'synthetic and consented avatars only'. HeyGen's paid plans include a custom avatar of your own likeness.
5. Watch each finished clip on your phone at full speed. If the lips, hands or text look wrong, regenerate or cut it. One bad frame can make the whole video look fake.
6. Export everything into one folder per week, named like '2026-W48_video1_hook-a.mp4', so editing day is quick.

**Done when:** You have usable footage for all five scripts in one weekly folder, and every face in it is yours or someone who gave you written permission.

**Watch out:** We couldn't confirm exact Higgsfield menu names or the credit cost per clip from public pages. The product changes often and model availability depends on your plan (cheaper plans get fewer models, so check what yours includes). We describe these steps generically for that reason. HeyGen's pricing page lists custom avatars by plan but doesn't spell out its consent process. Expect a verification step and follow it.

## 4. Captions, AI labels and the 'own face only' rule

**Goal:** Caption every video and label AI content correctly on YouTube, Instagram/Facebook and TikTok before you post.

**Time:** about 15 minutes. **Needs:** YouTube Studio, CapCut, Instagram, TikTok

**Steps (the path that works):**

1. Burn captions in for Reels and TikTok, since many people watch with the sound off. In CapCut on mobile, tap Captions, then Auto Captions, choose the language and tap Generate. Fix every misheard word, especially names and prices. Check CapCut's current plan, because its own guide lists auto captions as a Pro feature.
2. On YouTube you can also rely on free automatic captions. Review them in YouTube Studio under Subtitles, pick the video, and edit the lines that are wrong. YouTube itself says auto-caption quality varies.
3. On YouTube, label realistic AI. During upload, find 'AI use' in the Attributes section and choose Yes if the video is realistic AI-made or altered content, such as an avatar of you or a realistic generated scene. Choose No for scripts written by AI, captions, colour fixes, or cartoon-style visuals.
4. On TikTok, before posting, open More options (...) and turn on the AI-generated content setting for any realistic AI images, audio or video. TikTok requires this for realistic AI content and may add an auto-label you can't remove.
5. On Instagram and Facebook, add Meta's AI label when you share photorealistic AI video or realistic AI audio. Meta says penalties may apply if a required label is missing.
6. Keep a one-line consent record for anyone other than you who appears in AI-altered footage: who agreed, to what, and when. If you don't have that, don't make the video.

**Done when:** Every video this week has corrected captions and the right AI setting on each platform, and the only AI likeness used is yours.

**Watch out:** We couldn't confirm Instagram's or Facebook's exact on-screen name for the AI label toggle from public help pages, so look for the AI label option in the sharing or advanced settings screen. The CapCut free vs Pro status for auto captions comes from CapCut's own article (Nov 2026) and may vary by region and app version.

## 5. The weekly routine and what to measure

**Goal:** Run the pipeline in one 2-3 hour block a week, schedule five Shorts, and use retention data to pick next week's ideas.

**Time:** about 15 minutes. **Needs:** YouTube Studio, YouTube Analytics, Claude

**Steps (the path that works):**

1. Block one weekly session, for example Monday 9-12. Spend 20 minutes on notes and Claude scripts, 60 minutes filming or generating, 45 minutes editing and captions, and 20 minutes uploading and scheduling. These timings are illustrative, and your first weeks will take longer.
2. Schedule in YouTube Studio. Click Create, then Upload videos, add the details and the AI use answer, then on the visibility step pick Schedule and set the date, time and time zone. Space the five videos across the week.
3. Post the same files natively on Instagram and TikTok, applying their AI labels where needed, rather than relying on cross-posting links.
4. After 7 days, open YouTube Studio, then Analytics, then Content, and choose the Shorts view. Look at the share of viewers who stayed to watch past the first seconds. That number tells you whether your hook worked.
5. For any video over 60 seconds, the audience retention report shows where people dropped off. Move the strongest point earlier next time.
6. Paste your top and bottom performer's hooks into Claude and ask: 'What's different about these two hooks? Write 5 new hooks in the style of the winner.' Feed that into next Monday's notes.

**Prompt: Weekly review prompt**

```
Here are last week's 5 Shorts with their hooks and one number each: the percentage of viewers who stayed to watch past the first seconds (from YouTube Studio).
[Video 1 hook] - [x%]
[Video 2 hook] - [x%]
[Video 3 hook] - [x%]
[Video 4 hook] - [x%]
[Video 5 hook] - [x%]

My business: [business type] for [ideal customer]. Treat this as a small sample, not proof. 1) In plain English, what's the most likely difference between the best and worst hook? 2) Suggest one thing to keep and one thing to stop. 3) Write 5 new hook options for next week on the topic [next week's idea], in the style of the best performer. Don't use hype words or invented statistics.
```

**Done when:** Next week's five videos are scheduled in YouTube Studio, and you've written down which hook won last week and why you think it did.

**Watch out:** YouTube says retention data typically takes 1-2 days to process, and the key moments highlights need videos of at least 60 seconds with at least 100 views. Judge trends over 4-8 weeks, not single videos. We couldn't confirm a Shorts-specific retention guide on YouTube Help, so the Shorts metric named here comes from YouTube's content performance help page.
